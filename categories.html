Origins of the Universe & Earth

From the first moments after the Big Bang to the formation of our home planet.

The Big Bang Theory

The Big Bang is the leading scientific model for the origin and evolution of the universe. Roughly 13.8 billion years ago, the universe began in an extremely hot, dense state and has been expanding and cooling ever since.

Key Ideas

  • Space itself expands; galaxies recede from one another (Hubble–Lemaître law).
  • In the first few minutes, protons and neutrons fused into light nuclei (hydrogen, helium, lithium) — Big Bang nucleosynthesis.
  • About 380,000 years later, electrons combined with nuclei to form neutral atoms. The universe became transparent, releasing the cosmic microwave background (CMB) we still detect today.
  • Gravity later pulled matter into stars, galaxies, and the large-scale structure we observe.

Supporting Evidence

  • Observed expansion of the universe
  • Cosmic microwave background radiation
  • Abundances of the lightest chemical elements
  • Ages of the oldest stars and galaxies consistent with ~13.8 billion years

The modern picture is often called ΛCDM (Lambda Cold Dark Matter): ordinary matter, cold dark matter, and dark energy (Λ) that drives accelerated expansion.

Evolution of Earth

Earth formed about 4.54 billion years ago from the solar nebula — a cloud of gas and dust left over from the formation of the Sun. Collisions of planetesimals built a molten world that gradually cooled.

Major Stages

  • Hadean Eon – Intense bombardment, formation of the early crust and possibly the first oceans. The Moon is thought to have formed from a giant impact.
  • Archean – Continents began to assemble; the first evidence of life (microbial) appears.
  • Proterozoic – Oxygen levels rose (Great Oxidation Event), enabling more complex life.
  • Phanerozoic – The last ~540 million years: explosion of multicellular life, mass extinctions, and the rise of humans.

Earth’s atmosphere, magnetic field, and liquid water have made it unique in the solar system as the only known home of life. Plate tectonics continually reshapes the surface and recycles carbon, helping to regulate climate over geologic time.
